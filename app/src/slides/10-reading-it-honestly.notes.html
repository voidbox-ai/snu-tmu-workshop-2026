A few things worth saying about that.

First, the asymmetry. Two events crossed from the physical side into the virtual one; zero crossed the other way. The coupling we designed was bidirectional, and in practice one of the two directions never ran at all. The virtual exhibition was never able to act on the physical one — it could only watch.

Second, plainly: the second layer never formed. We laid it over the geometry and it did not take.

Third, the limitation, because I want to be accurate in this room. This tells you that our interaction layer was not used. It does not show that interaction is impossible. One event, five days, one department, and no control condition.

But at some point we stopped asking why people didn't use what we had built, and started asking what they did instead.
