And then the part we cared most about: an interaction layer. Most of our design effort went here, so let me be specific about what we actually built.

A guestbook — made to cross in both directions.

One direction. Someone visiting the online exhibition writes a note, and we copy it out by hand into the guestbook standing at the booth in the hall. There is no screen — the plan was that one of us would read it and write it down, so a visitor in the room finds it in the same book, in the same handwriting, as everyone else's.

The other direction. Someone in the hall writes a note by hand, on paper, the way you always have at an exhibition. They scan a QR code at the booth, photograph what they wrote, and it goes up on the online exhibition, beside the work it belongs to.

The reasoning behind both was the same. Something happening in the hall should change the virtual exhibition, and something happening in the virtual exhibition should change the hall. If the two spaces can act on each other, they stop being an original and a copy and become one event.

That is the second layer — our attempt to lay events back over the geometry.
