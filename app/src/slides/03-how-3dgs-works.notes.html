Very briefly, how 3D Gaussian Splatting works.

The premise is simple: photograph one object from many directions, and its three-dimensional form can be estimated. Cézanne built his mountain the same way — not from a single fixed viewpoint, but out of many separate glances held on one canvas. 3DGS does something close to that, with thousands of them, optimized.

Concretely: you walk through a space with a camera. Structure-from-motion estimates where each photograph was taken. The system then fits a cloud of translucent ellipsoids — Gaussians — each with a position, a shape, an opacity, and a color that changes with viewing direction. It renders that cloud from a known camera, compares it to the real photograph, and adjusts. Tens of thousands of times.

In the 3DGS output data — no mesh. No surfaces. No geometry. It reconstructs how a place looks, not what it is made of.
