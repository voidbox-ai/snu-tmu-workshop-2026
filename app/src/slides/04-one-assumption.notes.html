All of it rests on one assumption: that the scene did not change while you were photographing it.

Here is a playground we scanned. Everything is sharp — the slide, the railing, the paving. Except this. That is a person who walked while we were filming. He broke the assumption, so he was not reconstructed as an object; he was baked into the scene as a smear. What survives is that he was there. What is lost is where he was.

So this medium is good at space on the condition that events are excluded. And a community is not geometry — geometry is only the substrate it sits on. What makes it a community is a second layer laid over the physical one: the events. People meeting, someone presenting, someone stopping in front of a drawing for five minutes.

Which gave us a straightforward thing to test: can we add that layer back on top? That is what the first project was.
