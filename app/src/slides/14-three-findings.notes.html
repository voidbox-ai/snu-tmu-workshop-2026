Three findings from this.

One. Presence before synchronization. We spent our design effort on the coupling; what carried the project was the fidelity of simply being there.

Two. We measured the wrong thing. We built metrics for interaction — the metric that mattered was how long the space stayed open. And here is the sharp version: I can tell you exactly how many people visited the virtual exhibition. Eight hundred and thirteen. I cannot tell you how many visited the physical one. We never counted. That asymmetry is what a digital twin actually gives you — it makes presence countable. It is also the trap, because it is precisely what was countable that we optimised for.

Three. Not simultaneity, but asynchrony. Nobody wanted to be in two places at once; they wanted to be in one place, later. What joined the online and offline participants was not shared time. It was a shared place that stayed open.
